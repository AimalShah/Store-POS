1. Unit — aggregation maths (test/reports-accuracy.test.js)

computeSalesSummary — exact aggregation over real sales

UNIT Seeds categories/products through the live API, posts real sales, then calls computeSalesSummary({}) and asserts the arithmetic.

  • Sums paid sales by category, payment method and product — 2 sales (Cola×2@5 + Burger×1@10, then Cola×1@5). Asserts saleCount=2, itemsSold=4, subtotal=25, tax=2.5, totalSales=27.5; Drinks revenue 15 (3 units), Food revenue 10; cash 22 / card 5.5; best-seller Cola qty 3 / rev 15.
  • Excludes held orders (status 0) and refunds (status 2) — a paid sale (Cola×2) plus a held order (Cola×99) and a refund (Cola×3). Asserts only the paid sale counts: saleCount=1, totalSales=10, items 2.
  • Respects the date window — one sale dated 2020, one dated today. Queries a 2019–2020 window (expects the 2020 sale) and a 2024–now window (expects today's sale). Proves old sales never leak into current reports.

computeBestSellers — ranking by units then revenue

UNIT Cola (5 units @5) and Burger (5 units @10) tie on quantity; Burger wins on revenue. Asserts ranked[0].name === 'Burger' (rev 50) and ranked[1] === 'Cola' (rev 25).

X / Z shift reports — reconcile to the shift sales

UNIT Opens a shift (float 50), posts two shift-scoped sales (cash 10 + card 5).

  • X report — totalSales=15, cashSales=10, cardSales=5, saleCount=2, refundCount=0.
  • Z report (after close with countedCash 60) — expectedCash=60 (float 50 + cash 10), actualCash=60, difference=0.
  • X report counts refunds separately — a paid sale plus a refund; asserts saleCount=1, refundCount=1, refundTotal=5.

computeKpis — UI dashboard maths

UNIT Feeds synthetic transactions to computeKpis.

  • Derives sales=22, orders=1, aov=22, profit=12 (=(5−2)·2+(10−4)·1), marginPct=(12/22)·100, heldOrders=1, lowStock=1.
  • Handles an empty period without dividing by zero — aov=0, marginPct=0, orders=0. This is the guard against a phantom trend spike.

2. Behaviour — real API end-to-end (qa/features/reports-accuracy.feature)

GHERKIN Drives the live HTTP API the same way the UI does.

  • Sales summary reflects tax, discount and totals exactly — Acc Cola ×2 @10, discount 5, tax 2, total 17. Asserts summary.saleCount=1, subtotal 20, discount 5, tax 2, totalSales 17, byCategory count 1, byPaymentMethod count 1.
  • A split-payment sale is summed across both methods — cash 6 + card 4. Asserts totalSales 10, byPaymentMethod has 2 entries (6 and 4).
  • Best sellers rank by units sold and revenue — Best A (3×5), Best B (3×20), Best C (1×30) etc.; verifies ranking order.
  • Shift X/Z reconciliation — shift-scoped sales reconcile exactly (mirrors the unit X/Z block at the API level).

3. End-to-end — rendered UI (e2e/reports.spec.mjs, e2e/catalog.spec.mjs)

PLAYWRIGHT Launches the real Electron app, runs first-run + till setup, seeds two known paid sales through the API, then asserts what a human actually sees.

  • Dashboard KPIs reflect the real sales totals — seeds Rs10 (cash, Cola×2) + Rs9 (card, Cola×1 + Fries×1) = Rs19.00, 2 orders, profit Rs12.00. Asserts the KPI cards via data-testid: kpi-today-s-sales contains Rs19.00, kpi-orders contains 2, kpi-profit-margin contains Rs12.00.
  • Reports view shows the true aggregated totals — navigates to Reports and asserts Total Sales = Rs19.00, Items Sold present, and the QA Drinks / QA Food category breakdown renders.

Supporting e2e: e2e/app.spec.mjs (boot, sign-in, Dashboard KPIs visible) and e2e/till.spec.mjs (full till → cart → discount → hold → pay flow, stock deduction).

Front-end form tests — add category & product via the UI

PLAYWRIGHT Unlike the Gherkin layer (which POSTs form-encoded data to the API), these specs drive the actual React forms in the Catalog view — filling inputs, opening the shadcn category Select, and clicking the real Add category / Add product buttons.

  • Adds a category through the front-end form — opens the Catalog view, switches to the Categories tab, types into #cat-name, clicks Add category, and asserts the new category name appears in the list.
  • Adds a product through the front-end form and assigns a category — creates a category via the UI, reloads so the product form's category dropdown is freshly populated, then fills #product-name, #product-price, #product-cost, selects the new category from the #product-category Select, clicks Add product, and asserts the product is listed.

Note: #product-quantity is conditionally rendered only when Track inventory is checked, so the product test leaves quantity at its default rather than toggling that checkbox.
